--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HEADLINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The framing that these are not three independent OCR bugs is right, but the
shared cause is NOT alignment. I measured the warp residual, and on C01/C03 the
homography lands within +/-3 pixels of the blank (about 0.1 of a form row)
across the whole page. Alignment is working.

The shared cause is that the atlas is treated as ground truth by three
consumers but was never validated against the blank PNG it claims to describe,
and every stage downstream of the crop is "accept whatever came back".

Concretely:
  * one function, _merge_extracted, silently collapses same-target fields
    last-wins;
  * one threshold, ink_fill_ratio > 0.12, fires on the printed checkbox
    outline itself;
  * one keyword fallback, _after_label, feeds printed form-label text into
    real model targets.

Each of those alone is enough to produce all three reported symptoms.

I ran the real pipeline over the six fixtures in
backend/core/tests/fixtures/handwrite/ to get measurements rather than
inference. Everything below cites either code or that run.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. FORM CLASSIFICATION: C01 vs C02 vs C03 vs CW 05 vs COW 2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There are TWO independent classifiers and their disagreement is never resolved.

TEXT CLASSIFIER
  classify_text                        backend/core/scan_templates.py:66
  keyword table CLASSIFY_KEYWORDS      backend/core/scan_templates.py:36

  Uppercases the full-page OCR blob, counts substring hits, takes the single
  highest-scoring form. Confidence = min(0.95, 0.35 + 0.2 * hits).

  The keyword lists are badly unbalanced:
    c01  has 5 keywords              scan_templates.py:52
    c02  has 2: ['C02', 'C 02']      scan_templates.py:53
    c03  has 2: ['C03', 'C 03']      scan_templates.py:54

  So a C02 page tops out at one hit and 0.55 confidence. That is exactly what
  the fixture run produced for c02_adult.jpg.

VISUAL CLASSIFIER
  identify_form_page                   backend/core/scan_align.py:220

  Loops every blank page in blanks.json, tries 0 and 180 degrees
  (scan_align.py:240), calls match_blank, picks the highest inlier count, and
  rejects everything if the best is under 14 inliers (scan_align.py:248).

HOW THEY ARE WIRED TOGETHER
  backend/core/scan_ocr.py:427-438

      form_type, form_conf = classify_text(text)
      vis_type, vis_page, vis_warp, vis_inliers, vis_failed = \
          identify_form_page(working, hint=form_type)
      if vis_type and not vis_failed:
          form_type, form_conf = vis_type, min(0.95, 0.45 + vis_inliers / 80.0)

  Two problems:

  (a) The text guess is passed in as `hint`, and the hint RESTRICTS the visual
      search to blank keys starting with that code (scan_align.py:228-231).
      A wrong text guess therefore narrows the visual matcher to the wrong
      form. The unrestricted re-scan only happens if the hinted best is under
      14 inliers (scan_align.py:248-250).

  (b) form_confidence is a pure function of inlier count, not of agreement
      between the two classifiers. That number is not a confidence in any
      useful sense - see the C03 measurement in section 2.

2. ALIGNMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The chain is: contour-based page extraction -> a claimed deskew -> ORB +
BFMatcher knn ratio test + RANSAC homography onto the blank PNG.

  crop_document                        scan_align.py:86
    Downsamples to 1100 px, Canny + findContours, accepts the first 4-point
    approxPolyDP covering more than 20% of the frame.
    MEASURED: failed on 2 of 6 fixtures (c01_members_a.jpg, c02_adult.jpg),
    returning the photo untouched.

  deskew_and_contrast                  scan_align.py:32
    Effectively dead code. It inverts the grayscale and takes
    np.column_stack(np.where(gray > 0))       scan_align.py:40-41
    which selects every pixel that is not pure white - i.e. essentially the
    whole photo - so minAreaRect returns the frame's own angle, which fails
    the abs(angle) < 15 guard at scan_align.py:49.
    MEASURED: fired on 0 of 6 fixtures.
    The coordinates are also (row, col) rather than (x, y), so the angle would
    be computed in a transposed space even if it did fire.

  _homography_to_blank                 scan_align.py:162
    Shrinks both images to 880 px for ORB, requires 12 good ratio-test matches
    (scan_align.py:194) and 10 RANSAC inliers (scan_align.py:202), then warps
    to the blank's native size.

IS LANDSCAPE-SHOT-AS-PORTRAIT HANDLED, OR ONLY EXIF?

  It is handled structurally, but not correctly. It is not EXIF-only.

  maybe_rotate_to_template             scan_align.py:62
    Compares the scan's aspect ratio to the blank's. Returns [scan] when they
    agree, or [rotate(90), rotate(270)] when they differ. Combined with the
    0/180 loop in identify_form_page, all four orientations are reachable.

  EXIF is separate and applied via ImageOps.exif_transpose at
    scan_ocr.py:108, scan_ocr.py:142, scan_ocr.py:273,
    scan_align.py:21, scan_align.py:33

  The problem is that inlier count cannot distinguish those orientations.
  Probing each rotation directly:

    c03_mpilo   as-is 132   rot90 128   rot180 132   rot270 127
    c02_adult   as-is  10   rot90  10   rot180  11   rot270   8

  For C03, ALL FOUR rotations produce a "successful" homography with
  near-identical scores. These forms are dense tables; ORB descriptors on line
  intersections repeat across the grid, so RANSAC finds a self-consistent
  mapping of the grid onto a shifted or rotated copy of itself.

  The tie-break then falls entirely to _title_upright_bonus
  (scan_align.py:146), a RapidOCR pass on the top 22% of the page awarding 40
  points per keyword hit. So page orientation is decided by whether an
  upside-down header happens to yield a substring match. And the 0.95
  form_confidence reported for c03_mpilo is derived from 128 inliers that a
  180-degree-flipped page also earns.

  For C02 no rotation clears the gate, so match_blank returns (None, 0, True)
  and the page yields NO ATLAS FIELDS AT ALL. Note also that the only probe
  scoring failed=False was `as-is`, which maybe_rotate_to_template excludes
  because the orientations differ - though at 10 inliers it would have been
  garbage anyway.

RESIDUAL ERROR WHERE ALIGNMENT DOES SUCCEED
  Measured by cross-correlating ink profiles of the warped page against the
  blank, in eighths of the page:

    fixture            global dy    worst mid-page band
    c01_household        +1 px        +1 px  (0.03 rows)
    c01_members_b         0 px        +1 px  (0.03 rows)
    c03_mpilo            +1 px        -3 px  (0.14 rows)

CONCLUSION FOR THIS SECTION
  C02 never aligns. C03's orientation is decided by a coin flip. When
  alignment does succeed it is accurate. Anything wrong in a C01 or C03 field
  is therefore downstream of the warp.

3. ATLAS COORDINATE SPACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Boxes are NORMALISED 0-1 FRACTIONS OF THE OFFICIAL BLANK PNG.

  built by _box                        form_atlas.py:79
  documented at                        form_atlas.py:1-5

There is no CSS-pixel or print-point space anywhere in the atlas.

THREE CONSUMERS READ THE IDENTICAL fields_for() OUTPUT:

  SCAN         crop_box (scan_align.py:255) multiplies the fractions by the
               warped image size, and the warp target is exactly the blank's
               size (scan_align.py:216 warps to (w, h) taken from the blank at
               scan_align.py:167).

  PRINT        official_canvas.html:85-88 sets left/top/width/height as
               box[n] * 100 + '%' over an <img> of the same blank at
               width:100%.

  FILL CANVAS  OfficialFormCanvas.js:24-27, same percentages.

ANSWER TO "DO PRINT AND SCAN USE THE IDENTICAL ATLAS AND IMAGE SIZE?"

  YES. Print overlay and scan crops use the identical atlas in the identical
  coordinate space at the identical (normalised) image size. A print/scan
  mismatch is not possible by construction. If printed text lands in the wrong
  place, the atlas box itself is wrong, and the scan crop is wrong in exactly
  the same way.

  This is actually a useful property: one calibration pass fixes both.

RESOLUTION NOTE
  The blanks are rendered at "scale": 2.0 (official_blanks/blanks.json line 2),
  i.e. about 144 dpi, giving 1190x1684 portrait and 1684x1190 landscape.
  Because the warp target is the blank, EVERY FIELD CROP IS CAPPED AT THAT
  RESOLUTION regardless of photo quality.

  Measured crop sizes on the aligned C01 page:
    name / surname          644 x 32 px
    ID number               451 x 33 px
    checkboxes              25x26 up to 33x34 px

  Handwriting arrives at the OCR engine roughly 30 px tall.

  On these particular fixtures the incoming photos are already only about
  800x1280 (WhatsApp-compressed, 150-180 KB), so the 144 dpi target is not the
  binding constraint HERE - but it will be the ceiling for any properly-shot
  photo.

5. IS OCR RUN ON THE FULL WARPED PAGE OR ON EACH FIELD CROP?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BOTH, on every page.

  full page    _ocr_image(working)          scan_ocr.py:426
               Runs a full-page pass at --psm 6 plus RapidOCR. Feeds
               classification and the keyword fallback.

  per field    _atlas_fields                scan_ocr.py:309

  combined by  _merge_extracted             scan_ocr.py:373

THAT MERGE IS ONE OF THE CORE DEFECTS:

      for item in atlas_fields or []:              # scan_ocr.py:377-401
          target = item.get('target') or ''
          if target:
              by_target[target] = item

Same-target fields overwrite each other, last one wins.

Since _c01_member emits four separate member.N.race checkbox entries and six
caregiver.marital_status entries (form_atlas.py:104-124 and 151-167), EVERY
CHECKBOX GROUP IS SILENTLY REDUCED TO WHICHEVER OPTION IS DECLARED LAST IN
THE ATLAS.

In the fixture run that is exactly what happened, on all three C01 pages:

7. GIBBERISH FILTER - WHAT DOES IT ACTUALLY TEST?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

looks_like_gibberish                   scan_text.py:24

It is CHARSET AND SHAPE HEURISTICS ONLY. No dictionary. No confidence input.
No label list.

It returns False for anything under 5 letters, then flags:
  * no vowels at all
  * a run of 6 or more consonants
  * 12+ letters with 92%+ unique characters and no vowel-consonant-vowel
    pattern

REAL EXAMPLE OF A LABEL LANDING IN A REAL TARGET
  On c01_members_b.jpg the keyword path produced:
    caregiver.known_as = "'Nationallty Hencoo Date"
  The printed word "Nationality" stored as what someone is known as.

THE MECHANISM
  _after_label                         scan_templates.py:77
    Finds a label in the flattened full-page blob and returns the next six
    words. On a dense form the text following a label in reading order is very
    often the NEXT LABEL, and RapidOCR's reading order on these tables is not
    reliably left-to-right. The c02_adult.jpg blob came back in reverse column
    order:
      "...IDnunberi Nntionallty: IKnownAs: Surnamei Name: Pursonnel:
       Organisationi C02-ADULT"

  extract_fields applies this to caregiver.surname, caregiver.name,
  household.town, household.province and eight more targets for form types
  intake, c01, c02, c03 AND 'unknown' (scan_templates.py:132-197), so an
  unrecognised page still writes into household fields.

TWO FURTHER MIS-BINDINGS IN THE SAME FUNCTION

  (a) _first_sa_id (scan_templates.py:94) scans the WHOLE PAGE for any 13-digit
      run and assigns it to caregiver.id_number, caregiver.date_of_birth and
      caregiver.sex (scan_templates.py:124-130). On c01_members_b.jpg - a page
      containing only members 2, 3 and 4 - this attributed 2212280261081 and a
      2022 date of birth to the CAREGIVER.

  (b) Every C02/C03 atlas field targets caregiver.* (form_atlas.py:274-285)
      even though C03 IS THE CHILD BENEFICIARY FORM. A child's name and ID
      overwrite the head of household.

  up 25-38 px tall.

  _prepare_line_crop (scan_ocr.py:141) then upsamples to a 48 px floor and x3
  with a sharpen - inventing pixels rather than recovering detail.

    So the '4' read from c01_household.jpg is stored as a caregiver ID number
    FLAGGED AS VERIFIED, and it will not appear in the supervisor Verification
    queue.

(c) BUCKETS ARE FLATTENED LAST-WINS ACROSS ALL PAGES

    Keyed by target across the whole job, so uploading a C02 and a C03 in one
    batch means both write caregiver.name / caregiver.surname /

    child's identity overwriting an adult's, with no warning.
