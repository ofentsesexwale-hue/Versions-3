================================================================================
OVC-CaseFile - Scan Intake PHASE 4
The gibberish filter, and keyword-extraction mis-attribution
================================================================================

Scope: the four findings in the Phase 4 brief, and nothing else. Atlas geometry
- C02/C03 box sizes and targets, and the misplaced CW 05 checkbox boxes - was
not touched. That is Phase 5.

Result in one line: the form's own printed wording can no longer be saved as
somebody's answer, the flattened-page-text scraper is switched off on every
sheet the atlas can measure box by box, a 13-digit run found loose on a page
no longer names a person, and a half-read date box counts as unread.

================================================================================
3. WHAT WAS WRONG, AND WHAT WAS CHANGED
================================================================================

FINDING 1 - the filter had no idea what the form says
-----------------------------------------------------
looks_like_gibberish tested charset and word shape only. 'SURNAME' is a real
word made of real letters, so it passed, and so did 'FIRST NAME', 'ID NO',
'Date of Birth' and 'Passport Number'. Full-page OCR reads the printed labels
alongside the handwriting and nothing downstream could tell them apart.

Added core/form_labels.py, which builds the lexicon rather than listing it:

  - every label and tick caption in the field atlas for C01, C02, C03, CW 05
    and COW 2 (the atlas labels are measured off the official blanks, and its
    own docstring says "Labels follow the NPO PDF. Do not rename them")
  - the <span class="lbl">, <th>, <h3 class="sub"> and form-title text parsed
    out of the print templates for CW 05 and COW 2 (C01/C02/C03 print through
    the official-blank canvas, so they have no HTML template and the atlas is
    their only source)
  - the label wording the keyword extractor searches for, which moved out of
    scan_templates into EXTRACTION_LABELS here, so a label it hunts for is
    automatically a label it will refuse to hand back

That yields 136 distinct printed labels and a 180-word label vocabulary. Add a
field to a form and its label joins the lexicon; nothing is kept in step by
hand, and a test asserts the derivation both ways.

looks_like_form_label() rejects a candidate two ways. Outright, if it matches a
printed label ignoring case, spacing and punctuation. Or if it is short and
built entirely from label words - which is how the abbreviations actually
printed on C01 are caught without guessing at a list of them: 'ID NO' is
'id' + 'no', 'FIRST NAME' is 'first' + 'name', and both words are already in
the vocabulary from longer labels.

looks_like_gibberish now returns true for a label OR for keyboard smash. The
charset rules were not touched, only moved behind _looks_like_smash so the
sanitiser can reach them on their own. 'hgftrujyfdyt' is still gibberish,
'Hallie' is still a name, and there is a test asserting the old heuristics
still do their own job.

One thing this had to get right. A tick's answer IS a printed caption -
'Female', 'African', 'South African', 'Passport Number' are all captions on
the sheet and all legitimate values. The lexicon would have thrown away every
one of them, including the sex Phase 3 works out from an ID number. So the
lexicon stands aside for checkbox fields and for any target the atlas gives
tick options to (24 of them, derived from the atlas, not typed out). Those
fields still get the charset rules. As free text on a name field, 'Female' is
still refused.

FINDING 2 - _after_label was a primary path on forms that don't need it
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
It finds a label in the flattened page blob and returns the next six words. On
a dense sheet that is usually the next label, and where RapidOCR's reading
order is not left-to-right - C02 comes back in reverse column order - it is
not even the right part of the page.

extract_fields now asks the atlas what it can read, through a new
form_atlas.atlas_coverage(code) returning (has measured geometry?, targets it
can read box by box). On a sheet with an atlas, scraping is off entirely. On
any sheet, a target with an atlas box is skipped even if the crop came back
empty - an empty crop means nothing was written in that box, which is an
answer, and better than a guess.

  c01        atlas covered, 77 covered targets   -> no scraping
  c02        atlas covered,  5 covered targets   -> no scraping
  c03        atlas covered,  4 covered targets   -> no scraping
  intake     atlas covered,  4 covered targets   -> no scraping
  cow2_note  atlas covered,  3 covered targets   -> no scraping
  process_note / assessment / hiv_risk / educational / unknown
             no measured blank                   -> scraping still available

The atlas decides this, not the caller, so extract_fields cannot be called in
a way that re-enables the bug. Note that a form can have atlas boxes and no
official blank to align them to - process_note is one - and in that case it
reads nothing from those boxes, so atlas_coverage reports no covered targets
and the scraper stays in play. Getting that wrong would have silently emptied
the process-note fields.

And even on an atlas-free sheet, _after_label now refuses a candidate that is
itself printed wording, so one label cannot answer another.

FINDING 3 - _first_sa_id named a person it had no evidence about
----------------------------------------------------------------
It scanned the whole page for a 13-digit run and wrote it to
caregiver.id_number, caregiver.date_of_birth and caregiver.sex. Nothing
checked whose section of the sheet the number came from. On c01_members_b it
put a child's ID on the adult and derived a 2022 date of birth for them.

On any sheet with an atlas, the number is now surfaced as an unassigned
reading - shown, noted, attributed to nobody, and derived from for nobody.
Sheets with no atlas keep the old behaviour, because scraping is all they
have; there, an invalid 13-digit number is now marked invalid with its
problems listed, matching Phase 3, and still derives nothing.

_first_sa_id also now prefers a number that passes every SA ID rule rather
than one that merely passes the checksum, which is the Phase 3 rule set
reaching the last place that had not caught up with it.

FINDING 4 - date boxes had no filtering at all
----------------------------------------------
Date-kind fields went through OCR and straight to staff. Added
normalise_date_value(), applied to date-kind fields and to any target ending
date_of_birth / date_joined / date_registered. A candidate must split into
three number groups (or one run of eight digits, for a date grid read as one
block), parse as a real calendar date in one of the three orders that appear
on these forms, not be in the future, and not be more than 120 years ago.
Anything else is unread. A two-digit year reads as a birth date, so '01-02-55'
is 1955 and not 2055.

Same rule as an SA ID after Phase 3: the whole thing or nothing.

6. WHAT IS STILL BROKEN (Phase 5)
================================================================================

Atlas geometry, untouched as instructed, and now the clear next thing. With
the label and date noise gone, what remains on the fixtures is almost entirely
boxes pointing at the wrong part of the paper:

  1. C01 printed-field boxes are off. household.province reads 'gAaIGNg',
     household.town reads 'pI esgorarig', household.ward reads 'ern ee',
     household.district reads 'ranao'. Those are crops of the wrong region,
     not bad OCR of the right one - the neighbouring boxes on the same row
     read correctly (House Number '2291', Street 'Nkululuthweni').

  2. C02 still fails alignment outright (c02_adult.jpg: align_failed=True,
     zero fields). It has 5 atlas targets against C01's 77.

  3. C03 has 4 atlas targets and reads two of them ('nAPio', 'Khany' for
     Mpilo Khanyi).

  4. member.N handwrite boxes on C01 read fragments of the wrong cell
     ('WE Poe ge', 'eR', 'eae' for relationship_to_head), and
     disability_description picks up table rules ('_ > Fee Oe eee |').

  5. The CW 05 misplaced checkbox boxes noted in Phase 2.

  6. RapidOCR reaching only twelve digits on two ID crops, from Phase 3 -
     also a crop-edge problem.

Also still open and not in any phase brief so far: the charset heuristics
accept some real junk ('pI esgorarig', 'oholoza', 'ern ee'). The brief for
this phase explicitly said not to regress them, and the prescribed fix was the
lexicon, so they were left exactly as they were. Most of those values come
from the mis-aimed crops in item 1, so Phase 5 may remove the symptom without
touching the filter.
